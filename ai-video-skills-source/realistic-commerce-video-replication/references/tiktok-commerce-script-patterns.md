# TikTok Commerce Script Patterns

Use this when a user provides high-performing commerce scripts, complains that a script is not Chinese enough, lacks hook/burst points, feels like explanation instead of selling, or asks for Douyin/TikTok-style product scripts.

## Core Correction

Do not write a feature explanation. Write a filmed event.

Weak script shape:

```text
Pain point -> product feature -> quality proof -> CTA
```

Strong commerce script shape:

```text
Someone is already using the product -> a concrete old problem appears -> the product changes the situation on camera -> proof is shown through action -> a reason to act now closes the loop
```

The viewer should feel: "I caught a useful scene happening," not "I am watching an ad explain itself."

## What The Sample Scripts Do Well

- Product enters immediately. The first seconds show rolling under a car, lifting a car, frying food, kneeling in a garden, or opening a kit.
- The speaker has a stance: "I thought it was useless, but I keep using it"; "our shop has several"; "this is a gift someone will actually use."
- The script builds an old-way/new-way contrast: cardboard on the garage floor vs creeper, manual jacking vs button lift, small fryer vs party-size double fryer, kneeling on dirt vs supported kneeler.
- Features are embedded in use scenes. "2-in-1" becomes "flip from lying board to stool." "Six wheels" becomes "move side to side under the car." "Heavy duty" becomes "a real adult sits or works on it."
- Numbers appear after proof, not before proof.
- Closing pressure is specific: seasonal deal, free shipping, just restocked, likely to sell out, link location, or "if you can still order it, it is in stock."
- The language is spoken, direct, and physical. It uses short clauses and real actions instead of abstract selling words.

## Opening Archetypes

Use one opening archetype per script.

### 1. Action First

Start with the product doing the most satisfying thing it can do.

```text
Do not start: "Today I recommend..."
Start: "Watch this roll straight under the car."
```

For fruit:

```text
Do not start: "This lychee gift box is high quality."
Start: "This box, I am not showing the packaging first. I will peel one first."
```

### 2. Reversal

The speaker begins with doubt or a changed opinion.

```text
I thought this was another tool that would sit in the garage. I was wrong.
```

For fruit:

```text
I used to hate buying fruit gift boxes online. Too many boxes look good and taste dry.
```

### 3. Professional / Backstage Proof

Show that the product is used by people who repeat the job often.

```text
Our shop has several of these because people keep reaching for them.
```

For fruit:

```text
When we pack this batch, the first step is not sealing the box. It is picking out the bad fruit.
```

### 4. Gift Regret

Make the buyer imagine the recipient actually using or eating it.

```text
If your dad works in the garage, this is the kind of gift he will keep using.
```

For fruit:

```text
If this is for your parents or a client, do not only buy a good-looking box. Let me show you the fruit inside.
```

### 5. Old Way vs New Way

Show the bad old workaround, then replace it with the product.

```text
Stop lying on cardboard under the car.
```

For fruit:

```text
Stop judging lychee gift boxes by the outside. The inside decides whether it is worth sending.
```

### 6. Emergency / High-Stakes Scene

Start where the product removes anxiety, embarrassment, or wasted effort.

```text
You do not want to discover you need this on the roadside.
```

For fruit:

```text
You do not want someone to open your gift and find dry, dark fruit inside.
```

## 30-Second Structure

```text
0-3s:
Product already in action. One strong sentence with an event, not a topic.

3-7s:
Name the old problem or buyer fear in plain language.

7-14s:
First proof action. Show the result on camera.

14-21s:
Second proof action. Show process, labor, or repeat use.

21-26s:
Scene payoff. Gift, emergency, family, shop, party, self-use, or saved effort.

26-32s:
Specific close. Use only true offer facts. If offer facts are unknown, close with a proof CTA such as "comment to see packing" or "ask me to film today's batch."
```

## Feature-To-Scene Rule

Never list a feature unless it is attached to a scene.

```text
2-in-1 -> flip it from lying board to stool during the same repair
Six wheels -> slide sideways under the car without crawling
Electric lift -> press the button and the car rises
Two baskets -> fry two foods at once for a gathering
Foldable -> carry it, hang it, store it after use
Thick cushion -> kneel on gravel/soil without knee pain
```

For fruit:

```text
Gift box -> open it, peel one, then place good fruit back into the box
Freshness -> peel, split, squeeze lightly, show juice on hand
Sorting -> remove soft, cracked, bruised fruit before packing
Origin/process -> hands, baskets, sorting table, box, tape, delivery stack
Taste promise -> one honest bite reaction, not exaggerated slogans
```

## Closing Rule

Only use urgency that is true or explicitly supplied.

Allowed when known:

```text
summer sale
free shipping
just restocked
limited stock
link in the lower left
```

When offer facts are unknown, use a proof-based CTA:

```text
Want to see today's packing? Comment the product name and I will film it.
Ask me for the current batch and I will show you the fruit before packing.
If you are sending it as a gift, ask me to film the box before it ships.
```

## Fruit Commerce Adaptation

For fruit or agricultural products, the script should usually choose one of these premises:

- "Do not trust the box first; trust the peeled fruit first."
- "The fear is sending a beautiful box with bad fruit inside."
- "The seller is willing to show the batch before packing."
- "Bad fruit is actively removed, not hidden under packaging."
- "The gift only works if the recipient's first bite works."

The strongest burst point is usually a physical proof moment:

```text
skin breaks cleanly
juice hangs on the hand
flesh looks bright
bad fruit is removed
box is packed after inspection
```

Avoid turning these into generic claims:

```text
fresh and sweet
premium quality
high-end gift
selected source
strictly controlled
```

## Failure Signals

Rewrite if any of these happen:

- The first line could be said without the product on camera.
- The script opens with a topic instead of a scene.
- The proof is a slogan, not a visible action.
- The speaker has no stance, doubt, identity, or reason to be filming.
- The CTA says "buy now" without a concrete reason or proof continuation.
- The same structure could sell any product after replacing the product name.
