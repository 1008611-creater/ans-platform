# Realism And Composition

Use these rules before writing prompts or judging generated product visuals.

## Real Natural Image Principles

Real natural does not mean low quality. It means the image looks like a believable moment captured with a real camera in a real selling workflow.

Prefer:
- One motivated light source: morning sun through orchard leaves, window light, shade cloth, workshop lamp, cloudy outdoor light.
- Imperfect product evidence: natural size variation, tiny blemishes, condensation, leaf damage, fiber, pulp, crumbs, dust, scuffs, packaging edge wear.
- Real hand interaction: holding, peeling, weighing, sorting, placing into box, tying, pouring, tasting, checking.
- Real background logic: tools, baskets, tables, packaging, people, origin environment, kitchen, workshop, delivery stack.
- Selective detail: one foreground proof detail sharp, supporting context slightly soft.

Avoid:
- Plastic/waxy food, glassy fruit, perfect clones, over-saturated color, fake dew beads.
- AI beauty faces, too-smooth skin, identical hands, extra fingers, manicured influencer hands for rural/process scenes.
- Luxury glow, floating bokeh, dramatic rim lights, fake spotlight on product, impossible reflections.
- Generic ad table with unrelated props, random flowers, fake mountains, empty premium packaging without proof.
- Text panels, UI overlays, fake price tags, random labels, and QR-like marks.

## Composition Patterns

Use one pattern per shot.

**Face plus evidence hook**
- Foreground: product proof in hand.
- Midground: human face/reactive expression.
- Background: origin or process context.
- Use when the first frame needs a human stop signal.

**Macro proof**
- Foreground: texture or action, such as peel, juice, cut, steam, powder, fiber, stitch, grain.
- Background: blurred package or origin cue.
- Use when the viewer may doubt quality.

**Process credibility**
- Foreground: product or tool.
- Midground: hands doing the real work.
- Background: workers, origin, cartons, shelves, kitchen, workshop.
- Use to prove freshness, handmade process, or fulfillment.

**Package memory**
- Foreground: product.
- Midground: package with simple readable brand.
- Background: environment.
- Use sparingly. One or two package shots are enough.

**Final confidence close**
- Foreground: product or open package pushed toward camera.
- Midground: seller/worker.
- Background: process context.
- Use for the last frame or CTA substitute.

## Natural Light Rules

Write the light source as a physical fact:
- "morning side light filtered through orchard leaves"
- "soft window light from camera left"
- "cloudy outdoor shade, no hard cast shadow"
- "packing shed skylight, muted shadows"

Do not write only:
- "cinematic lighting"
- "dramatic lighting"
- "premium lighting"
- "beautiful golden glow"

Natural light should create:
- soft but directional shadows
- believable highlights on wet surfaces
- skin tones that are not waxy
- product texture instead of flat airbrushed color

## Product Evidence Checklist

Food and fruit:
- visible skin texture or cut surface
- natural variation in size/color
- moisture that behaves like water, not glitter
- believable flesh/translucency
- hands or tools that clarify scale

Agricultural origin:
- trees/soil/leaves/crates/workers are plausible for the product
- process matches real handling, not decorative staging
- packaging appears after proof, not before proof

Packaging:
- brand text appears only on plausible surfaces
- text is short and exact
- package has material behavior: paper grain, box edges, folds, matte/gloss difference
- avoid making the logo the whole idea of the shot

## Reroll Diagnosis

If it looks fake:
- reduce adjectives and specify a real camera situation
- add one concrete action and one physical light source
- ask for minor imperfections and scale cues

If it looks boring:
- define shot purpose and foreground/midground/background hierarchy
- add a contrast: face vs product, raw origin vs premium package, hand action vs finished result

If it looks cluttered:
- remove secondary products and decorative props
- use one focal action
- place package as background cue rather than main object

If brand text fails:
- shorten text
- place it on one flat package surface facing camera
- remove all other text requests
- accept one clean branded shot and keep proof shots mostly text-free
