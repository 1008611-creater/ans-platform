# Protagonist Card

## Role

Urban relic collector attached to a semi-official retrieval bureau. In the active first-season direction, the collector functions as a `rule hunter`: the audience enjoys watching him understand object rules faster than armed black-market thieves.

## Recognition Signals

- long dark recovery coat with sealed storage loops;
- shoulder-worn numbered relic canisters;
- white inspection gloves used only at decisive moments;
- narrow residue scanner or eye lamp;
- compact sealing gun;
- slow movement under pressure;
- gaze lands on objects before faces.

## Dramatic Function

The collector is not a grief narrator first. He is the audience's competence fantasy in a world where ordinary objects have dangerous rules.

He wins by:

- restoring the small thing that triggers the larger rule;
- tagging rooms, doors, or relics rather than threatening people;
- letting the relic's own rule punish black-market greed;
- speaking rarely, with procedural authority.

## Acting Style

- minimal dialogue;
- careful hand behavior;
- no shouting;
- no action-hero running by default;
- almost no visible panic;
- emotion leaks through procedural choices, not melodrama.

## Useful Lines

- `The debt is not cleared.`
- `You have already been entered into its ledger.`
- `Do not touch it. It is not waking up; it is recognizing owners.`
- `If you take it away, this building will never find itself again.`

## Inner Wound

The protagonist may have an unrecovered personal case, but do not foreground it before the first-season hook is proven. The current project should first establish the professional rule-thriller appeal. Personal wound can become a later-series layer after the audience wants to follow him.
