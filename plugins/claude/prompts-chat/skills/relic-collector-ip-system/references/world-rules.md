# World Rules

## Core Rule

Some ordinary objects become `anchored relics` after disaster, disappearance, collective use, violent severance, or unresolved departure. They are not magical prizes. They are ownership systems with triggers, memory, and debt.

## Why Relics Cannot Just Be Taken

- the object remembers where it belongs;
- the object may remember who touched it;
- unauthorized removal can trigger return paths, loops, exposure, or debt collection;
- black-market resale turns the thief into part of the relic's ledger;
- the bureau exists to classify, seal, return, archive, or preserve the object in place.

## Relic Grades

### C-level: Single-person relic

Usually affects one room, one person, or one routine.

Examples:

- key returns to its lock;
- lunchbox returns to its table;
- umbrella returns to the door where someone waited.

### B-level: Household or relationship relic

Usually affects a family, a route, or a group of bonded objects.

Examples:

- radio broadcasts the last shared channel;
- toy car follows a child's remembered route;
- doorplate traps the thief in the wrong ownership loop.

### A-level: Public relic

Usually affects a building, block, or public ownership chain.

Current first-season example:

- old tabletop fan: building-level ownership switch.

## Fan Rule

The fan does not summon treasure. It restores ownership.

- if the child's bus card leaves the fan guard center, the chain destabilizes;
- if an unauthorized person switches the fan on, stolen goods start returning;
- wherever the fan directs wind, misplaced objects seek their correct place;
- the more relics a thief has stolen, the heavier their debt becomes;
- taking the fan out of the building would break the building's ownership chain.

## Collector Workflow

1. enter sealed site;
2. identify the active relic and trigger conditions;
3. observe what the relic is trying to restore;
4. stop black-market interference;
5. seal, archive, release, return, or preserve in place;
6. if the relic is a public ownership switch, prefer on-site sealing over removal.

## Taboos

- do not remove an A-level public relic before identifying the connected chain;
- do not let black-market handlers keep touched relics;
- do not recover bonded relics separately;
- do not treat emotional residue as the only rule;
- do not falsify residue or ownership tags.

## Visual World

- deglamorized near-future sealed districts;
- rain-worn concrete, sealed apartment corridors, municipal tags, black-market tools;
- no neon-overload cyberpunk;
- no monster apocalypse;
- strong municipal, archival, forensic, and salvage textures.
