## Validated Reusable Patterns

1. Asset-first, not adjective-first.
   - Prompt text assigns work; assets provide visual evidence.
   - Separate character identity/wardrobe, scene-space, prop, storyboard/first-frame, and style duties.
   - More references are not automatically better; contradictory or irrelevant references reduce control.

2. Scene continuity begins with a space master.
   - Establish room layout and stable landmarks before extracting camera angles.
   - Scene master/overhead/angle board controls spatial relations; character board controls identity.
   - In this workspace, any repair or reroll uses approved whole-image Image2 channels, never unauthorized local pixel editing.

3. Visual reference responsibilities are explicit.
   - Sketch/storyboard: blocking, action direction, occlusion, composition.
   - Character board: face, hair, age, wardrobe identity.
   - Scene board: architecture, furniture, light direction, palette.
   - True first frame: current camera, composition, center action, initial continuity state.

4. Camera direction is a relationship, not a style adjective.
   - Specify angle, shot scale, focal-length behavior, foreground/midground/background, subject gaze/action, and dramatic purpose.
   - Eye level supports equality/intimacy; high angle can reveal predicament/space; low angle increases weight/presence. These are choices, not universal emotional laws.

5. Lighting must be motivated.
   - State source, direction, target, contrast, shadow retention, fill/catchlight, color, and emotional function.
   - Side-back/rim light separates subject; side light shapes conflict; hard light structures product imagery; near-prop light motivates local illumination.
   - Avoid copying exaggerated sample values as universal defaults.

6. Motion is layered visible change.
   - Body path and weight shift.
   - Secondary reaction of hair, wardrobe, accessory, or prop.
   - Micro-reaction such as gaze, blink, breath, fingers, jaw, or shoulders.
   - Environment/light response and sound connection when supported.
   - Keep one primary action per short shot.

7. Opponent scenes use blocking, not standing dialogue.
   - Define initial relation, discovery/trigger, distance change, gaze/occlusion, one character action, the other reaction, and who changes whose route or objective.

8. Empty/insert shots must carry story information.
   - Environment, prop, or emotional insert must clarify time/place/relationship/emotion or create a deliberate transition.
   - Formula: subject + current state + one dynamic detail + motivated light + simple camera movement.

9. Voice is structured performance.
   - Age feel + timbre + pace + emotional baseline + speaking habit.
   - Add breath, pause, stress, volume, and sentence-ending behavior.
   - Multi-line dialogue follows an explicit time-ordered emotional progression; punctuation supports pacing but is not the sole control.

10. Long-video continuity is designed before generation.
    - Prefer completed beat boundaries, planned overlaps, reaction/insert bridges, or video continuation where the approved provider supports it.
    - A final still frame preserves appearance/composition only and does not carry velocity, inertia, gaze transition, or camera motion.

11. Character design uses identity plus state.
   - Face/age/proportion establish identity; wardrobe, grooming, accessories, and wear establish status and story state.
   - Avoid full-face imitation of real people or celebrities. Use visible structure and original character design.

12. Realism is visual evidence, not a quality adjective.
   - Replace `真实`, `电影感`, and `高级` with concrete evidence: natural skin variation, hairline and flyaway hair, garment tension/folds, practical imperfections, motivated light direction, light ratio, color temperature, camera distance, and focal behavior.
   - When a character render becomes plastic, diagnose identity, light, camera, and detail separately. Do not add blanket smoothing or denoising language.

12. Skill quality comes from testing and revision.
    - A complete-looking Skill is not proven until real outputs expose failures and reruns demonstrate improvement.
    - Convert repeated failures into small route rules, prompt contracts, and machine-checkable QA where possible.

## Do Not Promote Without Evidence

- Tool-specific claims about nanobanan, Midjourney, Seedance, video extension, reference counts, or parameter values.
- Any claim that a prompt or asset layout guarantees identity consistency.
- 360/720 panorama extraction as a default production route.
- Local screenshot repair, local image patching, local compositing, or local text overlay for protected production/reference images.
- Exact copyrighted/personality imitation recipes.
- Long negative-prompt walls or copied sample prompts as universal templates.
- Storyboard as a mandatory default when the user/project did not request it.
- Provider submission, cost consumption, reference authority, or delivery from tutorial guidance alone.
